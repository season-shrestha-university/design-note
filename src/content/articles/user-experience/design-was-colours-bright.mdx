<div class="note__poem">

<p>
  I thought design was colours bright,
  <br />
  Pretty buttons, spacing right,
  <br />
  Fancy fonts and shapes that shine,
  <br />
  Making everything look just fine.
</p>

<p>
  So I picked a colour, picked a font,
  <br />
  Moved a button to the front,
  <br />
  Made it bigger, made it glow—
  <br />
  “Look!” I said, “Now watch it go!”
</p>

<p>
  But then a user came to see
  <br />
  And asked, “Where am I meant to be?”
</p>

<p>
  “Where do I click?”
  <br />
  “Where should I go?”
  <br />
  “I don't quite understand this flow.”
</p>

<p>
  I looked again at what I'd made,
  <br />
  The colours perfect, lines all laid.
  <br />
  It looked so nice, it looked so neat—
  <br />
  But somehow, it missed the beat.
</p>

<p>
  So I started asking, <strong>“Why?”</strong>
</p>

<p>
  Why is this button sitting here?
  <br />
  Why does this message cause a fear?
  <br />
  Why is this page so hard to use?
  <br />
  What does the person want to choose?
</p>

<p>
  And slowly then, I came to see
  <br />
  That design is more than what we see.
</p>

<p>
  It's not just colour, type, or space,
  <br />
  Or making something pretty in place.
</p>

<p>
  It's understanding what they need,
  <br />
  Helping them along with ease.
</p>

<p>
  The colours matter,
  <br />
  the fonts do too,
  <br />
  The shapes and spacing matter too.
</p>

<p>
  But they're only part of what we do—
  <br />
  There's always more beneath the view.
</p>

<p>And then someone asked me,</p>

<p>
  <strong>“But who wrote this?”</strong>
</p>

<p>
  <strong>Well... ChatGPT did.</strong>
</p>

<p>
  And does that make the lesson less true?
  <br />
  Does it change what the words can do?
</p>

<p>
  If a machine helped shape the rhyme,
  <br />
  Does that make the thought lose its time?
</p>

<p>
  A good thought is still a thought,
  <br />A useful lesson can't be bought.
</p>

<p>
  A little poem can still bring cheer,
  <br />A little knowledge can still stay near.
</p>

<p>
  We don't read Shakespeare just for the name,
  <br />
  Or praise the words because of fame.
</p>

<p>
  We read because the words still flow,
  <br />
  And make us feel and make us know.
</p>

<p>
  Words written hundreds of years ago
  <br />
  Can still make our hearts laugh or slow.
</p>

<p>
  They can make us question,
  <br />
  make us see,
  <br />
  And find a little something
  <br />
  we didn't know could be.
</p>

<p>So perhaps the same is true here.</p>

<p>
  It doesn't matter where words start,
  <br />
  If they can teach, inspire the heart.
</p>

<p>
  From you, from me,
  <br />
  or from a machine—
  <br />
  The meaning can still remain unseen.
</p>

<p>
  <strong>Knowledge is knowledge.</strong>
  <br />
  <strong>Joy is joy.</strong>
</p>

<p>A good idea is still worth the try.</p>

<p>
  If it teaches you something,
  <br />
  keep it near.
</p>

<p>
  If it makes you curious,
  <br />
  follow it here.
</p>

<p>
  If it brings you joy,
  <br />
  let yourself smile.
</p>

<p>
  Because learning is worth it
  <br />
  every once in a while.
</p>

<p>And if it makes you stop and say,</p>

<p>
  <strong>“What is design, really?”</strong>
</p>

<p>Then perhaps you've found the clue:</p>

<p>Design isn't just what looks good to you.</p>

<p>
  It's what helps someone understand,
  <br />
  what helps them move,
  <br />
  what helps them through.
</p>

<p>
  And maybe this little poem,
  <br />
  written with a machine's help too,
  <br />
  has done exactly what good design
  <br />
  is supposed to do—
</p>

<p>
  <strong>It helped you see something new.</strong>
</p>

</div>
